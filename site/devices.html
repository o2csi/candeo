Devices

Candeo drives lit devices that speak HID over USB. A device is on this page once its protocol has been surveyed on the hardware, written down with the firmware it was checked against, and verified by reading the writes back — not once it has been made to light up one evening.

What a device carries

A device, reduced to its essentials, is three things — and the first two are data rather than code:

  • An identity — the vendor and product ids, and which interface of the composite device carries the lighting. Never the firmware version or the variant string: the same keyboard called itself v1.4 / Unkown Variant in 2024 and v1.5 / Quartz in 2026, and a binding that matches on those breaks on update.
  • A layout — the matrix, and which of its cells sit under a key. An effect is written against the layout, so it runs on any device that declares one.
  • Its reports — how a frame becomes bytes, and the checksum that makes the device accept them. This is the part that has to be surveyed.

A status byte says the class and the command were understood. It never says the values were right, which is why every fact above was checked by reading the write back.

And where each fact came from

A layout carries its own provenance, so a reader can tell a measurement from a drawing:

Firmware
The version, and the device command it was read by — never the USB revision, which is frozen in hardware
Surveyed on
A date, so a fact can be re-checked against the firmware of the day
Method
Seen on the bus, written and read back, or inferred from a sibling device
Variant
The locale layout: an ISO and an ANSI matrix do not carry the same keys
Sustained rate
A measurement with its date, not a claim
Origin, per field
Surveyed, convention, or inferred — an inferred value says so rather than passing for a measured one

Written down in docs/design/device-sdk.md, which prepares #34 rather than describing what exists today.

Not here yet

Everything else. Hardware coverage is what the project costs now: each device means one USB capture, on that device, by someone who owns it. No interface removes that, and a guessed layout is worse than none.

Device SDK (#34) is the way out: a surface a contributor implements to add a device without touching the core, and a captured frame replayed in a test — so a contribution can be reviewed by someone who does not own the hardware. If you own a lit device and can capture its bus, that issue is where to start; the survey method is written down to be repeated.
